\begin{abstract}
Sampling from high-dimensional and multimodal distributions is a fundamental challenge in modern machine learning applications, ranging from generative modelling to Bayesian inference. In this thesis, we propose a family of algorithms that address these challenges through a theoretical framework that enables highly accurate samples from arbitrary multimodal distributions, as well as scalable methods for sampling from very high-dimensional Gaussian distributions. First, we propose a stochastic calculus framework for designing scalable and accurate neural diffusion sampling algorithms. Additionally, we propose a unifying framework for the task of "conditional" generation, i.e., obtaining samples from probability distributions that satisfy additional constraints. We demonstrate the performance of these algorithms on tasks involving sampling from unnormalised distributions, as well as conditional generation for inverse problems and finetuning. Finally, we turn to the specific task of developing highly scalable Bayesian inference techniques by proposing algorithms to sample from high-dimensional Gaussian distributions, and novel algorithms to optimise the hyperparameters of Bayesian inference algorithms using samples. Through this lens, we unify inference and hyperparameter optimisation in Bayesian Neural Networks and Gaussian Processes, showing high-quality uncertainty estimates at Imagenet-scale. 
\end{abstract}
