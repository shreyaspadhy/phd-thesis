\begin{abstract}
Sampling from high-dimensional and multimodal distributions is a fundamental challenge in modern machine learning, from generative modelling to large-scale Bayesian inference. This thesis introduces a versatile family of algorithms, spanning variational inference, optimal control, and stochastic optimisation, to address these challenges. Firstly, we develop a novel hierarchical variational framework, rooted in stochastic calculus, that unifies diverse generative modelling and sampling techniques, including diffusion models and optimal transport, by framing them as the minimisation of a path-space divergence. This perspective leads to new state-of-the-art samplers like Controlled Monte Carlo Diffusions (CMCD). Secondly, we leverage Doob’s \(h\)-transform, a seminal property of stochastic differential equations, to establish a unifying framework for conditional sampling, yielding the efficient DEFT algorithm for tasks like constrained image generation and protein design.
While these initial parts focus on unifying frameworks where stochastic processes model transformations between distributions, the latter part of the thesis develops sampling-centric algorithms for a critical application: large-scale Bayesian inference. Here, stochastic processes, particularly Gaussian Processes (GPs), serve as flexible priors. Inspired by the success of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) in deep learning, we develop novel SGD-based algorithms for efficiently drawing posterior samples from high-dimensional Gaussians that arise in GPs, and pioneer techniques using these posterior samples for rapid GP hyperparameter optimisation. Extending these ideas, we connect Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) to GPs via the Linearised Laplace Approximation and introduce a sample-based Expectation-Maximisation algorithm for scalable BNN inference and hyperparameter selection. This unified approach enables high-quality uncertainty quantification and model selection for BNNs at an ImageNet scale, demonstrating the power of combining principled sampling frameworks with scalable optimisation.

In this thesis, we propose a family of algorithms that address these challenges: from a novel variational framework that enables highly accurate samples from arbitrary multimodal distributions, as well as scalable methods for sampling from very high-dimensional Gaussian distributions. First, we propose a stochastic calculus framework for designing scalable and accurate neural diffusion sampling algorithms. Additionally, we propose a unifying framework for the task of "conditional" generation, i.e., obtaining samples from probability distributions that satisfy additional constraints. We demonstrate the performance of these algorithms on tasks involving sampling from unnormalised distributions, as well as conditional generation for inverse problems and finetuning. Finally, we turn to the specific task of developing highly scalable Bayesian inference techniques by proposing algorithms to sample from high-dimensional Gaussian distributions, and novel algorithms to optimise the hyperparameters of Bayesian inference algorithms using samples. Through this lens, we unify inference and hyperparameter optimisation in Bayesian Neural Networks and Gaussian Processes, showing high-quality uncertainty estimates at Imagenet-scale. 
\end{abstract}
